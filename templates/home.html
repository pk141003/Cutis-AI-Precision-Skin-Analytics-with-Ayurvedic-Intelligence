Help Center

Common questions about skin cancer detection, AI analysis, and data safety.

What is CutisAI and how does it work?

CutisAI is an AI-powered skin analysis platform that examines images of skin lesions using deep learning models. It identifies patterns such as asymmetry, color variation, texture, and size to provide early insights into potential skin conditions.

Does CutisAI diagnose skin cancer?

No. CutisAI provides AI-based predictions and risk indicators only. It is not a medical diagnosis. A certified dermatologist must perform clinical evaluation and tests for confirmation.

How accurate is the AI analysis?

The AI model is trained on dermatology datasets to achieve high accuracy. However, results depend on image quality, lighting, and skin conditions. It should be used as a screening tool, not a final decision.

What types of skin diseases can be detected?

CutisAI can analyze conditions such as melanoma, basal cell carcinoma, actinic keratoses, benign keratosis, melanocytic nevi (moles), vascular lesions, and dermatofibroma.

When should I consult a dermatologist?

You should seek medical attention if you notice rapid changes in size, shape, or color, irregular borders, bleeding, itching, or any unusual skin growth. Early consultation is critical for serious conditions like melanoma.

How should I take a photo for accurate results?

Use natural lighting, keep the camera steady, focus clearly on the lesion, and avoid shadows or filters. A clear, close-up image significantly improves AI prediction accuracy.

Are my images and personal data सुरक्षित (safe)?

Yes. Your data is protected using secure encryption systems. Images are used only for analysis and are not shared without your consent. Privacy and confidentiality are our top priorities.

How often should I use CutisAI?

For general monitoring, once a month is recommended. For suspicious spots, track every 2–4 weeks to monitor changes over time.

Does CutisAI replace a doctor?

No. CutisAI is a support tool designed for early awareness and preventive care. It does not replace professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment.
